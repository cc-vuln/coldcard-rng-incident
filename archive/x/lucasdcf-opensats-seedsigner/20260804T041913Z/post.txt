I think some people on the 
@OpenSats
 team who voted against funding 
@SeedSigner
 genuinely believe they have valid reasons not to fund it.

Remember, NVK had only one vote. And before the board even decides, there’s a recommendation from a subcommittee. So this wasn’t a decision made by one person, even if NVK may have influenced how others viewed the project.

Those reasons might include concerns about potential security issues with SeedSigner, or simply a lack of confidence in the project’s maturity.

But I think that’s actually more reason to fund it.

Regardless of what they think about the project, a meaningful number of people in the community already use SeedSigner. If there are concerns about its security, that makes it even more important to direct resources toward making sure those users are protected.

And I don’t think they should wait for a new application from 
@KeithMukai
.

This could be part of a broader response to the Coldcard incident: not only funding existing contributors, but putting out an open call for security researchers and engineers willing to conduct in-depth security reviews of major hardware and software wallets like SeedSigner, BlueWallet, Sparrow, Muun, Electrum, etc.

Side note: we usually fund people after they come to us asking for support for work they’re already doing. But there’s no reason grants have to work only that way.

We could also borrow from the job-listing model: identify work we think is important but nobody is actively seeking funding to do, announce grants for it, and actively look for qualified people willing to take it on.
