Here is the JSON export of my Coldcard hack mapping analysis from the past few days.

This represents dozens of hours of on-chain investigation.

So far, imo, three main patterns appear to emerge:
1) an initial wave of five clearly scripted hacks, followed by UTXO consolidation. The bitcoin linked to these transactions remains untouched for now;

2) a much noisier and more erratic pattern: numerous addresses, repeated consolidations with no obvious rationale, and several questionable fund movements. Some bitcoin eventually reached:
-> a cluster linked to Chivo Wallet and Binance;
-> six transactions involving ChangeNOW;
-> one Bitcoin service using CoinJoin, with outputs later connected to Binance;

3) several additional waves resembling the first pattern. Once again, the bitcoin associated with these transactions has not yet moved.

My current working hypothesis is that there may be:
- a first attacker using scripts to execute transactions in batches;
- a second attacker behaving far less carefully, making privacy mistakes and moving funds in ways that are difficult to interpret;
- potentially one or more additional attackers (or possibly the first attacker again) who have not yet moved the bitcoin because they know the transactions are being closely monitored on-chain.

This remains a preliminary assessment, and attribution is still uncertain.

Since the latest hack-related transactions have not yet been consolidated, I am going to step back from the manual mapping for now.

However, I now have a mempool-monitoring system running 24/7, with alerts for any transaction involving addresses or UTXOs included in my map.
