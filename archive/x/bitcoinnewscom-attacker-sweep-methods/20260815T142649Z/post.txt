COLDCARD ATTACKER HAD SOPHISTICATED INTEL BUT CRUDE SWEEPING METHODS

New research from 
@PraveenPerera
 suggests the attacker appears to have identified vulnerable addresses, ranked them by how much bitcoin they held, and then swept them in batches from largest to smallest.

But the actual sweep software appears surprisingly crude.

One address had 225 spendable UTXOs. The attacker took exactly the newest 200 and left the oldest 25 untouched, including one worth 0.16 BTC. 

That exactly matches the default 200-record limit of a blockchain API investigated by the researcher, suggesting the attacker may have simply failed to load the next page.

The software even spent a 294-sat UTXO that reportedly added roughly 2,040 sats to the transaction fee, meaning it cost substantially more to spend than it was worth.

That supports the author’s theory that the tool was constructed by someone who understood account-balance systems  better than Bitcoin’s UTXO model.

Despite apparently recovering the victims’ full seeds, the attacker left at least 75 BTC sitting at other addresses derived from those same seeds.

And the biggest mystery remains 153 stolen addresses containing 132.95 BTC. 

Researchers still cannot reproduce the seeds behind them, raising the possibility that the attacker had access to private device or candidate data that isn’t publicly available.
