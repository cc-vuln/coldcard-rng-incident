This reply to 
@callebtc
 was one day before the red team initiative began.

Kimi K3 went open weights on July 27th. I had pinged people about how powerful Kimi K3 was. We were red teaming our own infrastructure as it launched to see what it could find.

After the Cold Card hack, it became quickly evident that most people who maintained software were not using frontier AI tools for defensive security.

For a couple dollars in credits, you can look at any open source codebase to see what is found. There are a lot of rough parts around the edges, but there is one thing for certain, the world of cyber security has changed forever.

What we are doing is not particularly special, for $10-30 you can likely scan most small/medium code bases and take a look for yourself. There are great tools like 
@opencode
 we've been using to have access to many models at once.

The bag guys will do this (or already have!) and your security posture has to change.

Have a security.md file in your repository, this guarantees the cleanest way to handle responsible disclosure.

We are entering a new world of cybersecurity where anyone for the price of a nice meal can interrogate a code base. I'm not sure any of us understand the long term ramifications. 

If you are interested in supporting the effort you can donate to the 
@OpenSats
 red team fund.
