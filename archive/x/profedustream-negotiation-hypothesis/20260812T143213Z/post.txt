 Alert - Could the bitcoins stolen in the ColdCard hack be returned?!

Five days.

It's now been five days since the funds from waves 1 and 3 haven't moved.

Surprising for a theft of this magnitude.

Yet one hypothesis is gaining traction since the publication yesterday of a message inscribed in the blockchain via OP_RETURN:
 "OP_RETURN  ready to negotiate with Coldcard. T: startcourage hotcard@dnmx.cc"

Is the hacker seeking to negotiate the return of the funds in exchange for a ransom?

 Why does this hypothesis hold water?

As I explained on August 6, several elements suggest that the Lazarus Group is probably not involved in this attack. The reasons are detailed in my analysis cited at the end of this post.

Furthermore, an attacker truly wishing to profit from the funds would likely have already started to cover their tracks:
- swaps via THORchain;
- use of bridges or lending protocols;
- mixing;
- gradual dispersion of the funds across multiple networks and multiple addresses.

 However, none of that has been observed.

The bitcoins from waves 1 and 3 (likely stolen using batch-executed scripts written with Kimi K3) remain immobile on the consolidation addresses.

And all eyes are now fixed on those addresses; from Chainalysis to analysts in their underwear in their office at 35 °C (hello to the other miner buddies who keep mining, even in summer ).

In this context, anonymously proposing a negotiation via an address on 
http://
dnmx.cc (Dark Net Mail Exchange . Cold Card) would make a certain sense.
 But logical doesn't mean proof.

 What could happen next?

That's the big question. And the hope of several thousand COLDCARD users may partly rest on this negotiation.

In the most favorable scenario, between 1,000 and 1,400 BTC would be returned to COLDCARD in exchange for a ransom of several million.

 But recovering the bitcoins probably wouldn't mean that every victim immediately gets their funds back.

A long legal process could then begin:
- conversion of a portion of the BTC to fiat to fund the proceedings;
- legal and administrative fees sharply reducing the amounts returned;
- several years of steps to identify each victim and precisely establish their rights to the funds;
- need to prove physical possession of the relevant ColdCard in order to retrieve its uID and timer (two elements that allow deterministically determining the seed on the MK2 and MK3).

In the worst case, no negotiation succeeds, nothing is returned, and the bitcoins remain permanently out of reach for the victims 

 Does this message really come from the hacker?

That's where the main gray area lies. Because nothing today allows us to affirm it.

Indeed, this message was sent to an address attributed to the hacker, but not from that address.
 It therefore doesn't prove that its author controls the stolen funds.

It could perfectly well be an opportunist seeking to attract attention or impersonate the attacker.

This individual had already claimed the attack on behalf of the « Islamic Republic IR » on August 9, 2026, again via OP_RETURN:
"COLDCARD Hacked by Islamic Republic IR. Tel: startcourage E:hotcard@dnmx.cc"

They also:

Credible staging, elaborate troll, or genuine negotiation attempt?
 At this stage, impossible to say.
