Q: Does Bitkey have enough entropy? 

Less tech answer:
Bitkey is a 2-of-3 multisignature wallet, which means your funds are protected by three independently generated keys — one on your phone, one on your Bitkey hardware device, and one in our server-side secure module (WSM). No single key can move funds on its own. Each key is generated locally on its own device from a cryptographically secure random number generator, with no shared or coordinated knowledge, so the entropy protecting your wallet is the combination of three independent sources.
The app key is derived from a 256-bit seed produced by the operating system's cryptographic RNG (SecRandomCopyBytes on iOS and java.security.SecureRandom on Android).
The hardware key is generated entirely on your Bitkey device from a 256-bit seed produced by the hardware true-random-number generator (TRNG) inside the secure element of its main chip — a Silicon Labs EFR32MG24 with Secure Vault. The seed is drawn directly from the chip's Secure Engine (via sl_se_get_random), whose TRNG conforms to the NIST SP 800-90 standard, is used to derive the device's BIP32 key, and never leaves the chip.
The server key is generated inside an AWS Nitro secure enclave from a 512-bit master seed drawn from the enclave's cryptographic RNG, backed by the Nitro platform's hardware entropy.

Even more tech answer:
Bitkey is a 2-of-3 multisignature wallet, which means your funds are protected by three independently generated keys — one on your phone, one on your Bitkey hardware device, and one in our server-side secure module (WSM). No single key can move funds on its own. Each key is generated locally on its own device from a cryptographically secure random number generator, with no shared seed, so the entropy protecting your wallet is the combination of three independent sources. Because Bitkey's source is public [1], you can verify each of these directly:
The app key is derived from a 256-bit seed produced by the operating system's cryptographic RNG — SecRandomCopyBytes [2] on iOS [3] and java.security.SecureRandom [4] on Android [5] — as used in key generation [6].
The hardware key is generated entirely on your Bitkey device from a 256-bit seed [7] produced by the hardware true-random-number generator (TRNG) inside the secure element of its main chip — a Silicon Labs EFR32MG24 with Secure Vault [8]. The seed is drawn [9] directly from the chip's Secure Engine [10] (via sl_se_get_random), whose TRNG [11] harvests entropy from a thermal noise source with start-up health tests as required by NIST SP 800-90B [12], is used to derive the device's BIP32 key, and never leaves the chip.
The server key is generated inside an AWS Nitro secure enclave [13] from a 512-bit master seed [14] drawn from the enclave's cryptographic RNG, backed by the Nitro platform's hardware entropy [15] [16].
