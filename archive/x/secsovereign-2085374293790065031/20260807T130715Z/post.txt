Over 1,800 Bitcoin gone. The median victim held 0.4 BTC. These were not whales. These were everyday Bitcoiners. Your brother. Your sister. Your mother. Your father. People who saved carefully, did the research, bought the recommended hardware, kept it air gapped, and never touched a hot wallet. People who did everything right. The losses are still being counted.

The people who lost Bitcoin and happen to have a platform are publicly heartbroken and taking responsibility. The people who built their reputations recommending Coldcard and didn't lose anything are doing something else entirely. While the body count is still rising they are running the most self-congratulatory circle jerk this space has ever produced. Gratitude threads for cryptographers. Praise for ordinals repos. Blockstream PR. A 16-person red team posting hourly updates like they're liberating a city, dispatched after the massacre was already over.

These are not disinterested volunteers. These are people with equity positions, sponsorship revenue, and institutional relationships that depend on the space continuing to trust the stack they spent years promoting. The red team is not altruism. It is reputation insurance.

Concerns about the provenance of Coldcard's cryptographic primitives were being raised as early as 2021. By 2024, credible reports of entropy weaknesses had been delivered to Coinkite. By 2025, the specific library had been identified as not credible and replacement was explicitly recommended. At every step Coinkite waved it off. Now, after 1,800 Bitcoin has walked out the door, we are supposed to be grateful. So grateful. Thank you for dedicating your time and resources. Never mind that those resources were built on years of recommending a wallet that had been flagged repeatedly. Never mind the vested interest. Never mind the warnings that were ignored.

The failure was a build configuration error in a custom RNG library. Not exotic. Not sophisticated. Findable. Found. Reported. Dismissed. Then exploited.

Some victims already know. Some haven't checked yet. Some will open their wallet in the coming weeks and months and find out the most important thing they were told was a lie. All of them did everything right. They followed every instruction. They trusted the people who told them to trust nothing. And those people are trending.

What this moment demands is not a circle jerk. It is anger. It is hard questions asked on behalf of people who never had a platform to begin with and don't have one now. Who recommended this wallet and for how long. Who published guides featuring Coldcard as the default self-custody solution. Who collected sponsorship revenue from Coinkite while doing so. Who received the warnings and said nothing. They had years to do the verification work they are now performing in public. They did not. Performing competence after the fact does not erase the earlier recommendations or the ignored signals. It does not answer the questions the victims still need answered.

"Don't trust, verify" was printed on the tamper-evident bag the device shipped in. It was all over the marketing copy. It was repeated on podcasts, in documentation, across the entire ecosystem. The people who said it loudest are the same people who never verified the one thing that mattered. And now they are asking you to trust them again.
