The Coldcard incident has Bitcoiners asking the wrong question.

Instead of asking “Which wallet is impossible to exploit?” we should ask “How do we make stolen BTC practically worthless?”

Here’s a simple idea:

Create an open, opt-in Bitcoin Security Standard that every hardware wallet, software wallet, exchange, payment processor, custody provider, and Layer-2 solution can implement.

How it works:

 A cryptographically signed, publicly auditable registry of stolen UTXOs backed by verifiable on-chain evidence and a transparent dispute process.

 Governance through a decentralized multisig of independent organizations…no single company controls the registry.

 Wallets automatically synchronize the signed registry.

 Before signing a transaction, wallets perform deterministic UTXO ancestry analysis, tracing every input back to its origin.

 If any input descends from a flagged UTXO - even after dozens of hops, CoinJoin transactions, mixers, peeling chains, or complex transaction graphs - the wallet warns the user or refuses to sign, depending on its security policy.

 Exchanges, custodians, payment processors, and merchants perform the same verification before accepting deposits or payments.

 The registry is append-only, cryptographically signed, versioned, and fully auditable, making tampering immediately detectable.

 No hard fork. No consensus changes. 

Just an open security standard adopted across the ecosystem.

The objective isn’t to make Bitcoin censorable. It’s to make theft economically irrational.

If stolen BTC can’t be spent, deposited, sold, or accepted anywhere that adopts the standard - even after going through mixers…the incentive to steal it collapses.

Attack the attacker’s exit liquidity, not Bitcoin itself.

Thoughts?
