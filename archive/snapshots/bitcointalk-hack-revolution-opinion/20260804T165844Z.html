Develop a non-KYC stash

Some investors wish to keep their bitcoin investment private, so they seek out non-KYC bitcoin for several reasons. Non-KYC bitcoin can be helpful in purchasing products and services you don’t want tied to your identity, such as a VPN. Non-KYC bitcoin also means you’re not logged in a database somewhere for future targeting.

You can acquire non-KYC bitcoin in several ways. The first is through mining. If you mine successfully, you receive newly minted bitcoin via block rewards. This bitcoin is brand-new and has no other history on the blockchain, which is generally what third parties use to flag “tainted” bitcoin. Mining requires capital and skill to pull off, but non-KYC bitcoin is part of the appeal.

The second method for acquiring non-KYC bitcoin is to make a peer-to-peer purchase. This can be done in real life through an in-person exchange with someone you know. Give them cash and they give you bitcoin. This method comes with some personal security risk since in-person purchases sometimes lead to in-person attacks. There are also decentralized exchanges that allow you to purchase bitcoin online.

My recommended method for acquiring non-KYC bitcoin is to sell goods and services in exchange for BTC. To be clear, you’ll need to be receiving the bitcoin directly to a self custody wallet, not using a third party payment processor that has also likely KYC’d you.

Be aware that trading for non-KYC bitcoin isn’t completely anonymous in most cases because the counterparty likely knows at least a little information about who you are.

Post by: d5000 on August 04, 2026, 12:27:57 AM
Devices like Coldcard are somewhat in a "hybrid" space between self custody and non self custody. It is technically self custody because you generate your own keys, but you have to trust a hardware manufacturer, and in this case the trust was broken because of a "silly bug", as some have written.

Thus I think that the incident will not harm self-custody as such, but it could change it. More people would rely on a generic device, like a smartphone, or even on seeds stored on paper.

I've searched the web a bit and the current tendencies are not really clear:

- there are more Bitcoins now owned by centralized custodians (51%, up from 22% in 2022) - see this study (https://pluang.com/en/news-feed/penurunan-pemilikan-bitcoin-dengan-kepemilikan-sendiri)). The reason are the multiple newer custody products like ETFs and ETPs.
- awareness about self-custody instead is high (e.g. in this study (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260423585597/en/New-Research-Crypto-Users-are-Increasingly-Relying-on-Self-Custody-Solutions-to-Send-Receive-and-Grow-Assets) 66% were convinced that self-custody is the best way to hold).

I think we may be currently in a "anti self custody" wave indeed, but it will mostly affect insitutional investors who need vehicles like ETFs. While in the case of retail users, those who keep holding Bitcoin, will incresingly learn and store the Bitcoin in self-custody wallets.

As pooya87 wrote I don't think there are much more retailer newcomers now than some years ago which are driving the percentage of custodial wallets higher. Instead I think the peak of that behavior may have been the timeframe between the ICO craze of 2017/18 and the COVID pandemic, when many learnt about Bitcoin and crypto but first resorted to centralized solutions. I forgot the link but I've read a study that the percentage of new retail users has fallen since then, while the percentage of institutionals has grown.
