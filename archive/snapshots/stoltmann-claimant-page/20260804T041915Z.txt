If Bitcoin disappeared from a wallet whose seed was generated on an affected Coldcard device, protect any remaining assets, preserve the device and transaction records, and obtain legal and forensic guidance promptly. Depending on the facts, a victim may have claims involving warranty, negligence, misrepresentation, consumer-protection law, or a defective product but no claim or recovery is automatic.

What Was Reported About the Coldcard Wallet Vulnerability?

Coldcard hardware wallets are designed to keep Bitcoin private keys offline. That security model depends on a wallet seed being generated with enough cryptographic randomness that no attacker can realistically guess it. In late July 2026, Coinkite disclosed that a series of software-integration errors prevented the intended hardware random-number generator from contributing properly in certain firmware versions.

According to Coinkite’s updated technical backgrounder and migration guidance, the affected code path used a software fallback rather than the intended hardware source. Coinkite estimated that the effective search space was substantially below its intended 128-bit target, with different levels of risk across device generations.

Block’s Bitcoin Engineering and Security team independently analyzed the firmware and reported that the fallback could make seed generation reproducible under certain conditions. Block cautioned that practical exploitation depends on variables such as device identifiers, timing state, prior random-number calls, and derivation cost.

Public reporting associated the issue with a July 31, 2026 sweep of approximately 594 Bitcoin from roughly 500 single-signature wallets in about 25 minutes. These reports and the technical investigation are developing. No court has yet made findings about legal responsibility, causation, or damages.

Which Coldcard Devices and Seeds May Be Affected?

As of Coinkite’s July 31, 2026 update, users were advised to review the model and firmware that generated the seed. The manufacturer identified the following affected ranges unless sufficient independent dice entropy was added when the seed was created:

Mk3: seeds generated on firmware versions 4.0.1 through 4.1.9.
Mk4 and Mk5: seeds generated before standard firmware 5.6.0 or Edge firmware 6.6.0X.
Coldcard Q: seeds generated before standard firmware 1.5.0Q or Edge firmware 6.6.0QX.

Coinkite stated that TAPSIGNER, OPENDIME, and SATSCARD use different codebases and are not affected by this issue. It also stated that at least 50 fair, independent, private dice rolls added during seed creation may supply sufficient independent entropy. A strong BIP-39 passphrase can add a separate barrier, but a Coldcard PIN is not the same thing, and a weak or reused passphrase may be guessable.

The key question is when and how the seed was generated. Moving the same seed to a different hardware wallet does not cure weak seed generation because the underlying private keys remain derived from that original seed.

Why a Firmware Update Alone Does Not Fix an Existing Seed

A firmware update can correct the process used to create future seeds. It cannot add randomness retroactively to a seed that already exists. Coinkite therefore advises affected users to install the fixed firmware for their model, create a completely new seed, verify the new backup and receive address, send a small test transaction, and only then transfer the remaining balance.

Users should follow the current official instructions rather than improvising. A hurried migration can create a second loss through an incorrect address, an unverified backup, a lost passphrase, or disclosure of the seed to a fraudulent support service. Never enter seed words or a passphrase into a website or give them to anyone claiming they can check whether a wallet is vulnerable.

What Should You Do If Bitcoin Was Stolen?

Protecting remaining funds and preserving evidence may need to happen at the same time. Do not destroy or reset the original device merely to create a cleaner wallet history. If immediate action is required to protect assets, document each step and preserve the original hardware, packaging, backups, and records whenever safely possible.

Record the Coldcard model, firmware history, wallet fingerprint, purchase date, order confirmation, serial or bag number, and the date the seed was generated.
Save the affected public addresses, extended public keys if available, unauthorized transaction IDs, dates, times, amounts, destination addresses, screenshots, and watch-only wallet history.
Preserve communications with Coinkite, the seller, exchanges, support providers, forensic investigators, insurers, and law enforcement.
Create a written timeline covering device setup, firmware updates, seed imports or exports, use of dice rolls or a BIP-39 passphrase, and when the loss was discovered.
Do not publish the seed phrase, passphrase, private keys, device identifiers, or a complete evidence package on social media or public forums.

The FBI advises cryptocurrency victims to report promptly and provide transaction details such as wallet addresses, amounts, timestamps, and transaction hashes. Victims can review the FBI’s cryptocurrency complaint guidance and submit a report through IC3. Reporting does not guarantee recovery, but rapid reporting may help investigators identify where assets moved.

How Causation May Be Evaluated

A reported vulnerability does not, by itself, prove that it caused a particular loss. Attorneys and forensic experts may compare the seed-generation date and firmware range with the timing and on-chain pattern of the theft. They may also examine whether the wallet was single-signature or multisignature, whether a strong passphrase or independent dice entropy was used, and whether the seed was ever digitized, photographed, imported, or exposed.

The investigation should also consider alternative attack paths, including phishing, malware, compromised backups, exchange account takeover, physical access, or a phone-number takeover. This distinction matters because the responsible parties and dispute forum can change with the cause of the loss.

Potential Legal Claims After a Coldcard-Related Bitcoin Loss

The viable claims, defendants, remedies, and forum depend on the purchase documents, product representations, jurisdiction, and forensic evidence. Possible theories that counsel may evaluate include:

Breach of express warranty. Product descriptions, manuals, security statements, or written warranties may create enforceable promises. The precise language, reliance, limitations, and available remedies must be reviewed.
Breach of implied warranty. Depending on state law and the transaction, a buyer may argue that the device was not reasonably fit for its ordinary purpose. Disclaimers, privity rules, and notice requirements can affect the claim.
Negligence. A claimant may contend that reasonable secure-development practices, end-to-end testing, code review, or quality assurance should have detected the alleged random-number-generation problem. Duty, causation, economic-loss rules, and damages vary by jurisdiction.
Negligent misrepresentation or omission. Security representations may be relevant if they were inaccurate, incomplete, material to the purchase, and reasonably relied upon. The required proof differs by state.
Consumer-protection claims. State statutes may prohibit unfair or deceptive acts or practices. Some allow enhanced damages or attorney’s fees, while others impose pre-suit notice, reliance, or territorial requirements.
Product-defect theories. A hardware wallet combines a physical product with embedded software, creating jurisdiction-specific questions about design defects, software, economic loss, and recoverable damage. These claims require careful legal analysis rather than automatic application of traditional product-liability rules.

Will a Coldcard Claim Be Filed in Court or Arbitration?

A Coldcard hardware-wallet dispute is not automatically a FINRA arbitration merely because Bitcoin is an investment asset. FINRA jurisdiction generally depends on a dispute involving a FINRA member or associated person. Claims against a manufacturer, retailer, exchange, broker, phone carrier, or other party may instead proceed in court or in a contractually selected arbitration forum.

The analysis begins with identifying each potentially responsible party and reviewing the terms accepted during the purchase or use of the relevant product or service. For comparison, Coinbase fraud and account-loss claims may follow a different contractual path because they involve a custodial platform rather than a self-custody hardware device. Class-action waivers, choice-of-law clauses, warranty limitations, and arbitration provisions may materially affect strategy.

Can Blockchain Forensics Help Recover Stolen Bitcoin?

Bitcoin transactions are generally irreversible, but they remain visible on the public blockchain. Forensic investigators may trace stolen assets through subsequent addresses and identify transactions involving exchanges, custodians, payment services, or other entities that maintain identifying information. Counsel may then evaluate preservation requests, subpoenas, emergency relief, or coordination with law enforcement.

Tracing does not guarantee that assets will be frozen or returned. Speed matters because stolen Bitcoin may be split, moved across services, converted, or transferred into jurisdictions where legal process is slower. Victims should also be cautious of recovery scammers who demand advance cryptocurrency payments or ask for seed phrases.

What Determines Whether a Legal Claim Is Viable?
Whether the original seed was generated on an affected model and firmware version.
Whether sufficient independent dice entropy or a strong, unique BIP-39 passphrase was used.
Whether blockchain and device evidence supports a vulnerability-based theft rather than another compromise.
What security representations, warranties, disclaimers, and dispute terms applied to the transaction.
Which companies or individuals may be responsible and where they are located.
The amount of loss, available damages, insurance coverage, and practical ability to collect a judgment or award.
Applicable statutes of limitation, contractual deadlines, and notice requirements.
