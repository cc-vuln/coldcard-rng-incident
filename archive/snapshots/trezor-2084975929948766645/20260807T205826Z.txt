2. What if I generated my wallet on a Coldcard and later restored it on a Trezor?

You're still affected. Restoring it on a Trezor doesn't fix the weak randomness used when the wallet was created. Generate a new wallet on your Trezor and safely move your funds. [3/12]

3. If I send coins from an affected Coldcard wallet to a new Trezor address, are they safe?

Yes. Once confirmed, funds sent to a new wallet generated on Trezor are no longer controlled by affected Coldcard-generated keys. 

Simply restoring the Coldcard wallet on Trezor is not enough. The funds must be moved to a newly generated wallet. [4/12]

4. Are older models such as Trezor Model One and Model T still safe?

Yes. Trezor Model One and Model T remain perfectly safe to use.

They combine randomness from the device and the host to generate the wallet. Trezor Safe 3 and Safe 5 add randomness from the Optiga secure element as well, while Trezor Safe 7 also includes TROPIC01 (mixes four sources).

All models generate at least 128-bit entropy under default settings. [5/12]

5. Should I update my firmware and regenerate my Trezor seed?

Keep your Trezor firmware updated, but this incident does not require Trezor users to regenerate wallets created on a Trezor. If your wallet was generated on an affected Coldcard, create a new wallet and move your funds. [6/12]

6. How does Trezor’s wallet generation differ from Coldcard’s?

Trezor does not rely on one source of entropy. It combines entropy from the device and host, with additional independent hardware sources on Trezor Safe models. Entropy Check provides another safeguard by making parts of this process verifiable.

8. Will Trezor add more support for dice rolls or change its default wallet format?

We are studying the lessons from this incident and considering additional safeguards, including support for user-supplied entropy. We cannot commit to a specific feature yet.

The central issue in this incident was not simply the availability of entropy, but whether the firmware correctly used it. [9/12]

9. Would SLIP39 or multisig have prevented this issue?

Multisig may reduce risk if the affected Coldcard key is only one part of the setup and the remaining keys are secure, but the exact impact depends on the multisig threshold and configuration. An affected key should still be replaced. [10/12]

Historically, we used 24 words for the Trezor Model One, where you had to enter backup words to the desktop (in the randomized order suggested by Trezor, so it was still secure) to increase security.
