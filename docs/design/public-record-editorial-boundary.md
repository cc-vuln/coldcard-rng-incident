# Design: public-record editorial boundary

**Status:** implemented 9 August 2026. This refines the record-first decision
of 5 August and the original-research boundary adopted on 8 August.

## Decision

The project preserves, organises and explains the public record of the July
2026 COLDCARD predictable-RNG incident. It does not rank the incident against
other Bitcoin events, select legal rules for a reader, independently audit
firmware, or publish technical findings that no source has made public.

This is an editorial boundary, not a claim that those activities lack value.
They require different methods, review and accountability from an archival
record. Keeping them outside this site makes it clearer what a citation to the
record establishes.

## What belongs

- Captures and metadata establishing what a public source served and when.
- Attributed accounts of what parties, researchers, reporters and community
  participants published.
- Organisation that makes disagreements, revisions and chronology legible
  without selecting a winner.
- Explanations grounded in preserved publications or public source artefacts.
- Arithmetic on stated inputs when the inputs and method are visible.

## What does not belong

- Comparative significance rankings authored by this project.
- Independent firmware or binary verification presented as an incident
  finding.
- New technical inferences beyond arithmetic on published inputs.
- Selection or application of law to a reader's circumstances.
- General wallet-security guidance or personal risk classification.

Where a third party publishes any of those things, the publication may still
belong as attributed incident discourse. Holding it is not endorsing it.

## Presentation consequences

The landing page describes documented events and sends readers into the
record. Precise or disputed totals remain on deeper pages with their sources
and assumptions. Legal pages register terms, filings, solicitations, public
opinions and party statements; they do not construct this project's own legal
analysis. Technical pages explain the published incident accounts and public
patches, but stop where the record would otherwise become an independent
research report.

Material removed under this boundary was out of scope rather than necessarily
wrong. Its removal is recorded in `CHANGELOG.md`, not `corrections.toml`.
